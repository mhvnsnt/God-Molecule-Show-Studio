# AD RESEARCH — Cosmic/Weird Ad Grammar for God Molecule Promos

*Owner directive 2026-10-10: research the most popular ads of the last 20 years and make our ads/commercials better. This doc is the GOD MOLECULE application — show promos, trailers, and interstitial ads for the weirdest show on the network.*

## The campaigns that matter (2006–2026)

### 1. Skittles — "Taste the Rainbow" weirdness era (2007–2015)
A man with a rainbow beard being petted. A giant hand crushing a man's office. Zero product logic, total memorability. Skittles committed to the bit for a decade: the weirder the spot, the more on-brand.
**What we steal:** God Molecule promos should commit to the bit. The show is cosmic horror-comedy — its ads should feel like transmissions from inside the show, not commercials ABOUT the show. Weirdness is the brand; half-measures read as accidents.

### 2. Old Spice — "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" (2010) + Response Campaign
Absurdism with a straight face, single-take impossible transitions (boat → oysters → diamonds → horse). Then 180 personalized response videos in 3 days.
**What we steal:** impossible transitions are God Molecule's native language (the show is about reality breaking). A promo where the narrator's background melts through three realities in one take is both an Old Spice homage and on-bible. And: Kevin answering fan questions in-character is the Response Campaign with a trickster god.

### 3. Adult Swim bumps — surreal interstitial grammar
Deadpan text cards, non-sequitur humor, anti-ad posture. The network that made "not selling" the hardest sell.
**What we steal:** God Molecule interstitials should feel like AS bumps that went wrong — a calm card that glitches, a sentence that contradicts itself. The promo IS a micro-episode of the show's thesis (reality is negotiable).

### 4. Nike — mythic storytelling
Ordinary humans, mythic stakes, three words ("Just Do It"). The ad doesn't explain the product; it inducts you into the myth.
**What we steal:** God Molecule promos should induct, not explain. Never summarize the plot. Give the viewer a ritual, a phrase, a feeling — "born with the words of duty" energy. Mystery outperforms exposition for weird shows.

### 5. Apple — "1984" (1984, still the reference) / minimalism
One image, one idea, total confidence. The most famous ad ever made barely shows the product.
**What we steal:** a God Molecule promo can be one image (the green iris, the forehead portal) and one line. Restraint makes the weird hit harder.

### 6. Dollar Shave Club (2012) — controlled chaos + ruthless cutting
$4,500, founder on camera, every frame essential ("If it wasn't 100 percent essential, it was gone").
**What we steal:** weird doesn't mean sloppy. Every surreal beat must earn its seconds. Cut anything that doesn't disorient, delight, or convert.

### 7. TikTok ad grammar (2020–2026)
Hook (0–3s) → Body → CTA. Pattern interrupts. Start at the peak, not the buildup.
**What we steal:** a God Molecule promo should START inside the weird — no establishing shots, no "in a world." Open on the impossible thing. The hook for a cosmic show is a violated expectation.

## The God Molecule promo grammar
Per Bible A (production-stage ShowBible.tsx is canon), the show's engine is: the Void ritual is the straight man, Kevin is the parallel chaos; death is a menu; the forehead portal is real. Promos inherit this:
1. **The straight setup, then the wrong turn.** Open normal. Break it by beat 3. The break IS the ad.
2. **Never explain the cosmology.** A promo that explains the 14 realities is a lecture. A promo that shows one impossible thing is an invitation.
3. **Kevin is the spokesperson.** The trickster sells the trick. Deadpan wrong philosophy, never winking (the Xavier lesson from the research wave).
4. **Transmissions, not commercials.** Frame promos as found footage from inside the show's universe — a bump that glitches, a card that knows you're watching.
5. **One impossible image per promo.** The green iris. The portal. Pick one, hold it, end.

## Techniques to steal — checklist for every God Molecule promo
- [ ] Opens INSIDE the weird — no setup, no "in a world," violated expectation in the first 3 seconds.
- [ ] Straight setup → wrong turn by beat 3 (the Void ritual is the straight man).
- [ ] Kevin (or the trickster voice) delivers the pitch deadpan — never winks, never explains.
- [ ] One impossible image held long enough to burn in. Not a montage — one image.
- [ ] Feels like a transmission from the show's universe, not a commercial about it.
- [ ] Mystery over exposition: induct the viewer, don't brief them.
- [ ] Absurdity committed fully — half-weird reads as a mistake.
- [ ] Cut ruthlessly: every surreal beat earns its seconds or it's gone.
- [ ] Ends on ritual, not information: a phrase, a gesture, a portal — something the viewer repeats.
- [ ] Works silent: the image alone should unsettle/intrigue with sound off (most promos are watched muted first).
