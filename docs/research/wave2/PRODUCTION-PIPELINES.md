## OFF THE AIR — the one-man edit bay

**Software (Hughes, direct quote):** "I use Final Cut to edit, and After
Effects and Photoshop to manipulate. A couple compression softwares to do
the data-moshing thing, but not much more than that." — kittysneezes
interview, Sept 27, 2012.

**Company/crew:**
- Produced by Hughes's own company, **Million Monkeys Inc.**; network side:
  Williams Street / Adult Swim.
- Core crew is tiny: **Hughes edits every episode himself.** Co-producers
  **Cody DeMatteis and Alan Steadman** assist — specifically tracking down
  licensees and hard-to-obtain material.
- Executive producers: Keith Crofford, Mike Lazzo.

**Schedule:** ~4 episodes per year, 10–12 minutes each, airing in the 4am
graveyard slot. No published start-to-finish figure — episodes accumulate
over months via the bucket method; turnaround is elastic, not scheduled
(Hughes: "Coping" "just popped out" during lockdown).

**Commissioning in practice:**
- Artists found by Hughes trawling the internet (Vimeo, Facebook messages
  from his "Cheesepapa" screen name).
- The brief is **theme + trust, not specs** (animator Victoria Vincent: let
  artists "make something they actually care about and feel passionately
  towards").
- Commissioning increased over time for three stated reasons: web content
  got harder to pan for "real nuggets," **the budget increased**, and
  platform personalization made discovery worse.
- From Season 7, **guest curators parallelize output** (Wham City, Ghosting.tv,
  Maddie Brewer, Mike Diva, Vernon Chatman, ColeKush...).

**Music licensing:** tracking down licensees is "the most difficult aspect
of the process" (Juxtapoz). The Adult Swim banner helps get consent; only a
few artists respond with reluctance. Treatment ethic: each piece "treated
with protection" — never exploiting or mocking anyone. (The original pilot
concept included Adult Swim's own shows; Hughes had to cut them because the
network would still need licenses for derivative works — and "the show
really opened up" once they were gone.)

**Budget:** no per-episode dollar figure exists publicly. Two data points:
(1) Hughes pitched Mike Lazzo in person and got "here's ten bucks, go make
it" — the first year was green-lit episode by episode because it "cost very
little to make"; (2) the budget has since increased enough to fund more
original commissions. No numbers ever stated.

**Origin:** late 2009 — a video to play behind bands at the Adult Swim
Carnival. Prelinger Archives + web sources, effects "until you didn't quite
know what you were looking at," "some good music underneath to give me
something to edit to" — "basically a video mixtape that you would project
at a party." That mixtape became the pilot.

**Music-first doctrine:** "finding the right music to go with the visuals,
or vice versa"; transitions "really help keep it moving." He broke from
Liquid Television's long segments to make "one larger event rather than a
series of smaller events" — effects and transitions are "a huge part" of
the theory.

**For God Molecule:** the one-man edit bay is the model for the splat-world
assembly — one editor, Final Cut/After Effects-class tools, datamosh-style
transition plugins, music-first cutting. The bucket method is the
reality-generation pipeline. Guest curation is how output parallelizes
without growing headcount.

## XAVIER: RENEGADE ANGEL — the game-studio scam

**Team (PFFR):** Vernon Chatman (co-creator/writer/EP/director, voice of
Xavier), John Lee (co-creator/writer/EP/director, voices), Alyson Levy
(co-creator/writer/supervising producer→EP, voices), Jim Tozzi
(co-creator/EP, voices). Lisa M. Thomas: producer, all 20 eps. Adult Swim:
Nick Weidenfeld (the exec who said "Make 10 episodes" after PFFR mailed him
a Tozzi painting), Keith Crofford.

**Animation: Cinematico, San Francisco** (not NY — corrected per the primary
interview). Day job: videogame animation. Full CG in **Autodesk Maya**; some
motion-capture (vendor unknown). Chatman verbatim: "for very little money,
they do a feature film's worth of [animation] in **10 months**" — and "The
scam that we pulled is that they do animation for videogames, so they don't
have to get Hollywood money." No public budget figure — only "very little
money."

**Pipeline order (Chatman & Lee, verbatim):** "We write and then do the
voiceover, and then do storyboards and then send it their way."
Voice-first, from the creators' mouths. The four PFFR members voice nearly
everything. Direction was remote — "we hook up iChat to watch over them
every minute" — with absolute control at a distance: "They have all the
freedom in the world to do exactly what we tell them to do." Some
Cinematico animators got Xavier-symbol tattoos.

**Schedule:** 4 months to write a season; ~2 weeks per script ("We spend a
lot of time on lunch, the fanciest lunch we can get"); 20 eps × 11–12 min,
Nov 2007–Apr 2009.

**Season 1 vs 2:** S2 is "Denser and Wackier" — faster pace, plots pivot
mid-episode, jokes more rapid, nearly every episode ends in apocalyptic
disaster. **"Damnesia You" (S2E9) is entirely fan-made** — winners of the
"Make Your Own Xavier" contest (14 credited; styles: puppetry, Atari 2600,
Garry's Mod, live-action, Yellow Submarine-style, Squigglevision), framed
as "a tape we found in the garbage." M. Wartella (Superjail!) did a 2-minute
Aztec-style 2D segment for S2's "Damnesia Vu." S2 finale: "Braingeas Final
Cranny" (Xavier runs out of desert; the arc-symbol is a Rorschach blot —
the series may be a mental patient's hallucination). Cancelled after S2
(low ratings); DVD Nov 10, 2009.

**For God Molecule:** the scam is the template — a game-CG-capable pipeline
(Blender, in our case) doing TV animation for non-Hollywood money.
Voice-first is the locked pipeline order. The fan-made episode is the
extreme form of theme-and-trust commissioning. Remote direction with
absolute control ("all the freedom to do exactly what we tell them") is the
model for distributed production.

## EASTERN MIND: TONG-NOU — the four-person auteur build

**Team:** four-person core, explicitly including Sato and his wife. Named
credits: directed/written/produced by **Osamu Sato**; music: Sato; **main
programming: Amiko Takama**; programming advisers Hidekazu Osawa, Taizo
Tsuruoka; executive producers Hiroshi Inagaki, Terry Tsutsumi; co-writer
**Hiroko Nishikawa** (scenario; her decade-long dream journal later became
LSD). ~11 total credits.

**Hardware/tools:** built for **Classic Mac OS** (Macintosh exclusive at JP
launch), later a Windows 16-bit port. Engine: **Macromedia Director** — a
CD-ROM multimedia authoring tool, not a game engine. Evidenced workstation
(same window, June 1993 book colophon): **Adobe Illustrator 3.2J on a
Macintosh Quadra 700**. Sato's method (from his 1993 guide): begin CG the
way you begin music — from a vague image; new ideas appear as you work; the
computer is the ideal tool.

**Schedule:** development began 1993; JP Mac release **May 21, 1994** —
roughly a 12-month build. Windows re-release Oct 21, 1995; NA release
(Sony Imagesoft) Dec 1995 after delays. A PlayStation port was planned but
fell through. Originally planned as a trilogy; abandoned mid-development.

**Publisher:** in 1993 Sato won the **Grand Prix of Sony Music Japan's
Digital Entertainment Program** — the leverage that got **Sony Music
Entertainment Japan** (a *music* company) to publish the game. For the US,
Sato framed it as an **"interactive CD-ROM experience" rather than a video
game**. JP release shipped with a **double-sided insert: a map of Tong-Nou
and a message in an invented Tong-Nou alphabet**. US launch: a San Francisco
launch party; box copy claimed "40 to 50 hours of game play."

**Budget:** no figure published anywhere. Flagged unknown, not zero.

**Music production:** Sato was an active techno-house musician during
development; the soundtrack is his working genre (old-school electro with a
new-age bent). **Game ⇄ album pipeline:** five tracks on Sato's album
*Transmigration* (Oct 21, 1994; the game's opening song is the album's
track 1). US release bundled 3 Transmigration tracks + the *Transcript* CD.
The soundtrack exists as a standalone artifact, not BGM.

**LSD comparison (1998):** bigger, more industrial — published by **Asmik
Ace** (a *film* company); credits name dedicated artists and a separate
designer; Sato moved from auteur-everything to producer/concept/music lead.
Music: ~500 sample-based patterns (pattern-library approach). Source:
Nishikawa's actual decade-long dream journal; shipped with soundtrack CD
*and a book of dream-diary excerpts*. PlayStation 3D first-person
exploration. Sato's framing (Vice, 2015): "rather contemporary art using
game consoles."

**For God Molecule:** the auteur-plus-one-programmer build is the proof
that a tiny team ships a world. The music-company-as-publisher story is the
model for the game ⇄ album pipeline as a business structure, not just a
creative one. The invented alphabet / map insert is the model for
physical-feeling paratexts (the Field Guide interstitial). The Director
"wrong tool" choice rhymes with our Blender-for-everything approach.

## The combined pipeline (what God Molecule actually runs)

| Stage | Source | God Molecule practice |
|-------|--------|----------------------|
| Writing | Xavier (2 wks/script) + Tong-Nou (dream-text) | Write → voiceover → storyboards → animation. Dream-journal writer for inner worlds. |
| Voice | Xavier (voice-first, creators voice everything) | Record Mars's voice before storyboards. Cadence drives timing. |
| Worlds | OTA (buckets + theme/trust) + Tong-Nou (four lands) | Per-reality buckets; four-land elemental taxonomy; theme-and-trust commissioning. |
| Assembly | OTA (one editor, music-first, transitions as geography) | One edit bay, music-first cutting, datamosh-style bridges. |
| Animation | Xavier (game-CG pipeline, Maya→Blender) + deliberate cheapness | Blender, stylized limits, the intentional-failure test. |
| Music | Tong-Nou (game ⇄ album) + OTA (music-first) | Soundtrack as standalone album, tracks debuting in-episode. |
| QA | Xavier (fan-made episode) + OTA (guest curators) | Theme-and-trust commissioning; guest curation parallelizes output. |

**Crewing truth (all three agree):** tiny core, huge output. Hughes: one
editor + two license-trackers. PFFR: four writer/performers + one game
studio. Sato: four people, one programmer. God Molecule's team shape is
already correct — the constraint is the method, not the headcount.
