# EASTERN MIND: THE LOST SOULS OF TONG-NOU — Deep Craft Research
## World-building, writing, and production study for God Molecule

**Wave:** God Molecule deep research wave 1 (owner 2026-10-09, standing directive).
**Purpose:** go deeper than the wave-0 pass (INSPIRATION-RESEARCH.md, PR #85) into
content, writing craft, and the actual 1994 production — so God Molecule's
world-building, season arcs, and production model can steal working methods.
**Canon anchor:** Bible A is #1 canon. Mars's forehead symbol = portal into his
own mind; the environment is consciousness manifesting. Kevin enters through
the forehead. Infinite worlds map onto four lands: Life, Time, Desire,
Dreaming + Central Mountain (the forehead core). Death = progression, not
reset. Season arc = nine lives/missions collecting soul-fragments. A
trickster-hub marketplace connects all realities (Kevin's territory).

## 1. Dialogue & Writing

### Who wrote it
The narrative was co-written by **Osamu Sato + Hiroko Nishikawa** (MobyGames
Chu-Teng credits confirm her as "Scenario" writer and co-author of the
original story). Nishikawa was Asmik Ace staff — her **decade-long personal
dream journal** later became the entire basis of *LSD: Dream Emulator* (the
game shipped with a book of excerpts). Sato brought the concept and visuals;
Nishikawa brought the *oneiric writing* — she is the dream-text person. Her
signature: surreal writing that treats the impossible as mundane.

### How the game talks
No voice acting; everything is subtitle text at the bottom of the screen. The
tone is **deadpan folktale** — poetic-didactic mission briefs, dry one-line
character bios, NPCs who state cosmic facts the way a shopkeeper states
prices. Nobody explains the weirdness; the game narrates it as logistics.

**The Mirror = the game's "scripture voice."** One mission-brief per creature,
in terse parable form (official English, via the Osamu Sato wiki):
- Rin: "When all of the five Magatamas have been found, your soul will be
  restored."
- Retsu: "No one is allowed into the Tree of Life, except the King of Life.
  But, only Retsu can open the gates of this Tree of Life."
- Zen: "In the Land of Dreaming, all outsiders who disrupt dreaming are
  disliked. Zen is the only one who can withstand the coldness and guard the
  ice walls."
- Kai: "When the Candles of Time are put out with the Water of Dreaming, life
  will come to an end. Kai, who possesses the Leaf Sack, is the only one who
  can draw Water of Dreaming from the Land of Dreaming."

The craft: every brief is a riddle that only fully makes sense *after* the
life is done — the writing withholds the punchline (Kai's candle is his own
life). The game refuses exposition and teaches by consequence.

### Rin is light-hearted and sensory, not philosophical
His actual lines (official English): "Frozen? Looks kinda dead." … "Munch,
munch. Ahhh, it's so delicious I can't believe my taste buds!" … "Gobble,
gobble. How fresh. I'm amazed." … "Slurp, slurp. Wonderful." … "The ceiling is
falling!" … "Uggg, it's Poison." The protagonist's voice is a **kid with his
mouth full** — a deliberate tonal contrast to the cosmic stakes.

### The Illustrated Book entries = the best writing sample
Every creature gets a dry, faux-encyclopedic entry with Likes/Dislikes:
- Tou: mechanical-apparatus fetishist with "long and thin" fingers and precise
  eye-glasses — but "cannot bear his very own physique," an aerodynamic body
  with "a hole right in the middle" and "long and winding neck" as an
  inferiority complex.
- Kai: "Dressing up in colorful flames turns him on. Burning red is his
  favorite color. Although his body is constantly up in flames, this doesn't
  necessarily mean he's a passionate one."
- Zen: "prisoner of the ice wall, gazing at the river flow and intersecting
  Magatamas are his only pastimes" … "To be confined within ice is
  heartbreaking. Hopes to literally 'break the ice' someday."
- Pang-xie (the crab): "His own legs are his favorite dish. No need to worry,
  they just keep growing back. Indeed, an eccentric creature." Dislikes: "No
  alcohol, please. One drink and he'll turn completely red and eventually go
  unconscious. When under the influence, he'll feed you all, one after another,
  with his private cuisine."

And the killer device: **characters come with their moral thesis printed on
them** — Retsu: "Born with the words of duty 'help the troubled.'" Zen: "Born
with the words of duty 'notify all change.'" Like toy packaging.

### How characters handle the weirdness: they don't
Exposition is a one-way gift: Tou-gyou's image just *tells* you where
magatamas are; Moku-gyou asks a quiz (who owns the eyeball — answer: Tou-gyou)
and opens the tree when you answer. Questions are asked calmly; absurd answers
are accepted. A mysterious bronze creature that says "Fun!" repeatedly and runs
across the screen is never explained — "It's all there in the guidebook."

## 2. Sato's Production

### Who he is
Born 1960, Kyoto. Father and grandfather were photographers of **Buddhist
art** — the spiritual imagery is inherited, not affectation. Age-10 visit to
Osaka Expo '70 left a deep impression. Trained at Kyoto Institute of Design /
Kyoto Saga Art College; worked at Mos Advertising, freelance 1988, founded
**OutSide Directors Company (OSD)** the same year. Stated influences: **Russian
avant-garde, futurism, Bauhaus** — plus a "boundless love for the computer as
a creative tool."

### Music career (he was a musician before a game dev)
Debut ambient cassette *Objectless* (1983). Active **techno-house musician** at
the time of Eastern Mind — the soundtrack is techno-house/electro because that
was his working genre. Discography: *Transmigration* (1994 — the Eastern Mind
album; the game's opening song is track 1), *Equal* (1995), *Linen Sampler*
(1997), *LSD & Remixes* (1998), *All Things Must Be Equal* (2017 — reworked
game themes). **Five Eastern Mind tracks appeared on *Transmigration*; the US
game release bundled 3 tracks plus the contents of the *Transcript* CD.**
The pipeline ran *both directions*: game ⇄ album.

### How Eastern Mind was made (1993–94)
Built by Sato + **three collaborators** (development began 1993); MobyGames
lists ~11 total credits. Sato personally did: direction, production, art,
character/CG design, writing (with Nishikawa), and all music. **Engine:
Macromedia Director** — a CD-ROM multimedia tool, not a game engine; the whole
game is essentially an interactive multimedia stack. Programmer: **Amiko
Takama.** Published by Sony Music Entertainment Japan — a *music* company.
Eastern Mind was treated as a multimedia art release, which is how a weird art
project got Sony distribution.

### His method
From his 1993 guide *The Art of Computer Design* (quoted in HG101): you begin
CG the way you begin music, from a vague image; new ideas appear in your mind
as you work; you don't create by following directions, you create because you
want to make something new; the computer is the ideal tool. HG101 on the visual
result: creatures are "a grossly modelized mash-up of simple geometrical
shapes that have no regard for the laws of anatomy or physics," as if made
"using automatic drawing, under the influence of mind-altering substances" —
between ethnic hippie craft, alien hieroglyphs, and procedural fractals. Kill
Screen: it's "a window into the recesses of Sato's mind."

**No interviews exist.** HG101 is explicit: "there exists no real interview
with Sato." The Obscuritory got one email from him (about the *Chu-Teng*
sequel being lost — his rough English: it has "already become the
discontinuance of making"). Sato to Vice (2015): "I find my creations are
rather contemporary art using game consoles."

### Production artifacts
Japanese release shipped with a **double-sided insert containing a map of
Tong-Nou and a message written in a Tong-Nou alphabet** — Sato invented a
script. US launch party held in San Francisco with Sato's work flown in from
Japan. The island is an altered scan of **Sato's own head**.

## 3. The Four Lands (Detailed)

Each land maps to one **wuxing element**, each ruled by a King, each holding
one force of Rin's soul. The five forces: **Earth (Source)** — Central
Mountain; **Metal (Eternity)** — Yui-wang; **Water (Change)** — Mon-chien;
**Wood (Life)** — Ming-ken; **Fire (Confrontation)** — Shi-chieng.

**MING-KEN — Land of Life (Wood).** Entered through **Tong-Nou's left ear** →
linear cobblestone tunnel → branching flat field → **maze-like forest** → the
Tree of Life (Mingke-shu). Contents: Moku-gyou (king) + partner; the
**Zoh-gaeru Family** (four frog-like creatures at forest dead-ends, who only
talk to Tou); Tabelinai (optional dead-end); Tou-gyou's emblem; a
lava-bubble-style eyeball-rock leading to the Phantom Marketplace. Also
contains **Sha-mo Desert**. Home lives: Tou (guardian of the tree) and Retsu
(the ant). Force: Wood (Life).

**SHI-CHIENG — Land of Time (Fire).** A vast **yellow plain — the Plains of
Chi-u — dotted with hundreds of candles**, each candle = one life on Tong-Nou;
when a candle goes out, that being dies. Center: the **Fire Tower of Time
(Tong-tah)**, housing a **Computer** (life-registration terminal — typed
ZO-U-GA-E-[RA/RE/RI/RO] inputs). Inhabitants: Ka-gyou, Ying & Yang, Ga-show,
T.B.C. Home lives: Kai (guard of the Candle of Life) and Gyou (performs the
Ritual of Time). Force: Fire (Confrontation). Note the anachronism the game
never blinks at: an ancient Buddhist candle-plain with a computer terminal
inside a fire tower.

**YUI-WANG — Land of Desire (Metal).** Entered through the **right ear — by
clicking the correct lava bubble** (wrong one = consequences). A main hallway,
a secret room hidden behind a statue, and a **Phantom Marketplace entrance
opposite the statue**. King-gyou rules; giving him the **Golden Flower** wakes
him and he leads you to the **Helix Palace Luo-shang — a winding tower built
inside the palm of King-gyou's hand**, a hexagonal helix of inward-facing
rooms, each room a living embodiment of a desire: **Tong-shi (Greed), Chi-fang
(Appetite), Pu-ryao (Immortality), Show-mi (Sexual Desire), Non-fai
(Destruction)**. At the palace roof: the Metal Magatama floating free, plus an
A-mong running a gambling game. Home lives: Sha and Jin. Force: Metal
(Eternity).

**MON-CHIEN — Land of Dreaming (Water).** Entered through a **waterfall**; a
lake ringed with **giant ice-crystal walls** and crystalline pillars. The
entrance tunnel has **Zen trapped in the ice wall** — clicking him makes his
face explode (he stays dead; it's pure gag). Leads to **Ping-chao, the Palace
of Dreaming** — which holds **shortcuts to every other land** (the hub inside
the hub). Also a rare Goh-a-mong encounter and a Chu-teng symbol warp to the
Central Mountain. Home lives: Byou and Zen. Inhabitants: Sui-gyou (king), Ah &
Ung (riddle-dragons at the Palace door), Mong-i, Mong-aru, Zuo-mong (the
living apparatus), Pang-xie. Force: Water (Change).

**CENTRAL MOUNTAIN (TONG-NOU) — Earth (Source).** Sealed gate requiring all
nine Nameplates; up the steps, Tou-gyou's image directs the magatama/katana
endgame; at the top, the **Octagonal Shrine Par-fang**, where statues of the
four kings have entrapped the Earth Magatama. **Twist:** Tong-Nou is not a
soul-devourer — it is an island that **purifies troubled souls**. The four
kings were using stolen soul-magatamas for their own ends; they trapped
Tou-gyou (king of the mountain) in the shrine and **tore him into eight
pieces, scattered across the world** — eight pieces, eight reincarnation lives.
Rin's soul was the only one strong enough to survive the kings; by restoring
Tou-gyou, Rin's soul becomes more powerful than before.

### Visual/musical identity between lands
The lands are visually distinct (forest-maze, candle-plain, gold-helix-tower,
ice-crystal lake) but the deeper differentiator is the **elemental system** —
every land's music, king, life-forms, and soul-force rhyme with its element.
Sato scored each land in his techno-house idiom with different textures (HG101:
old-school electro with a new-age bent, synthetic drums, mesmerizing pads,
krautrock echoes, progressive-house beats).

## 4. Mechanics (Detailed)

### The nine lives — what each one actually is
1. **Rin (human)** — the frame. Recover his soul.
2. **Byou** (Dreaming) — born with a **Wrench**; replace Zuo-mong's defective
   Eyeball of Dreaming with a fresh one harvested from inside the Tree of
   Life. Full quest: answer Ah & Ung's riddle ("Golden Flower"), distract
   Moku-gyou with an ant to enter the tree through his mouth, pass his quiz
   (eyeball's owner = Tou-gyou), descend to the roots, take the pulsing
   eyeball, return, wrench it in.
3. **Tou** (Life) — guardian of the Tree of Life, born with a **Disc of Wood**;
   register the Zoh-gaeru family's names + his own on the **computer in the
   Fire Tower of Time**; return the Registration Card to Moku-gyou.
4. **Sha** (Desire) — born with a **Bell of Gold**; collect four instruments
   across the lands; return them — either assemble the Little Box of Sound, or
   give them to King-gyou, who **takes them and gives nothing** (a joke about
   desire as theft).
5. **Kai** (Time) — born with a **Leaf Sack**; draw the Water of Dreaming,
   then extinguish the candle at the king's coordinates — and learn the candle
   was **his own life**.
6. **Jin** (Desire) — was supposed to serve King-gyou; **his body
   melts/collapses during reincarnation from the weight of Rin's soul**.
   Cutscene life. Instant Nameplate — the "life" is a gag about greed being
   structurally unsound.
7. **Retsu** (Life) — an ant. Born to open the Tree of Life's gates; gets
   distracted, is **grabbed and thrown at Moku-gyou, dies** — the impact makes
   Moku-gyou sneeze, which opens the gates. **The mission is completed by
   dying on someone's face.**
8. **Zen** (Dreaming) — ice-wall guard; spots an intruder, yells, **gets
   stomped to death**. Mission complete — he "notified all change." The player
   can later stomp Zen themselves as another incarnation.
9. **Gyou** (Time) — **burns himself alive** in the Ritual of Time, lighting
   the Candle of Time with flames from his head. Mission complete.

### Death-and-reselect mechanics
After the first death, you descend to the **roots of the Tree of Life** and
choose **one pair of eyes, one nose, one mouth** (2×2×2 = 8 combinations = the
8 creatures). You may re-die at any time to pick a different life;
**progression and inventory survive death** — dying is a menu, not a
punishment. No Game Over exists. "All that has been acquired will be inherited
to the next life."

### The five elemental magatamas
Rin's soul is shattered into five comma-shaped jewels (Metal, Water, Wood,
Fire, Earth), scattered across the lands and endgame. Placed into "the empty
shell of Rin's soul" at the end.

### The 49-day timer — it is NOT a clock
No documented day-countdown mechanic exists in any walkthrough. The 49 days is
**pure narrative framing**, drawn from the Buddhist **Bardo** (7×7=49 days
between death and rebirth). It pressures nothing mechanically — deliberately.
The game removes every standard pressure (no game over, no timer, no death
penalty) so the *only* pressure is spiritual: you borrowed someone's soul; act
like it matters. The masterstroke: **the only true Game Over in the entire
game is the Moon Water in the Room of Immortality** — drink eternal life and
you get an inescapable infinite slideshow of every scene. In a game where death
= progress, *choosing not to die is the only loss.*

### How it teaches without tutorials
(a) The Mirror's one-line parables; (b) the **Tong-Nou Illustrated Book**
(buyable/tradeable — its Likes/Dislikes are the actual hint system); (c) NPC
quizzes (Moku-gyou's eyeball question); (d) **death itself** — the first
half-hour teaches you the only winning move is dying. HG101: the game is
"purposely stripped of all logic" — and learning to *adopt Tong-Nou's wicked
surrealness as a regular thought pattern* IS the tutorial.

Sources: compu-lsd.com/w/Byou, /Tou, /Sha, /Kai, /Jin, /Retsu, /Zen, /Gyou,
/Nameplate, /Mirror · obscuritory.com

## 5. Environments & Navigation

### The head-world geography
Tong-Nou is Sato's own green head floating in an intersidereal void. Entry
points are orifices: **left ear → Land of Life; right ear (correct lava
bubble) → Land of Desire**; earholes and cheek cavities lead into the land
network; clicking random spots on the head triggers things like a drill
bursting from the skull leaking green blood, the head vomiting eyeballs, and a
cheek containing dozens of screaming eyeballs. The four lands are "a nervous
system of its own" (HG101).

### Travel between lands
Primarily re-enter through the head's orifices, but the game plants
shortcuts: the Palace of Dreaming (Ping-chao) holds shortcuts to the other
lands; a Chu-teng symbol in Mon-chien warps to the Central Mountain; a
Chu-teng symbol under the Tree of Life also warps to the mountain. **Ping-chao**
functions as the inner hub.

### The Phantom Marketplace (Hou-ang)
Reached via **Eyeball-Stones** found in Ming-ken, Ping-chao, and Shi-chieng —
or a statue in Yui-wang. Marked by **eyeball mountains**. A deceptively linear
hallway of trading stalls; only one is occupied — **Mai-chiu**, the "able
merchant who manages the Phantom Marketplace Hou-ang alone. The Phantom
Marketplace Hou-ang appears and vanishes along with Mai-chiu's ever-changing
moods." Trading is **barter-only, with a published exchange card** (the wiki
documents the full trade table). His book entry: "Mai-chiu is a weirdo who
just loves the trading business. Therefore, the exchange regulations can be
quite unique." / He "dislikes customers who think money buys everything."
Trades serve as **alternate puzzle solutions and renewable sources of
single-use items** — e.g., the Amulet (one-use death shield) can be traded
*away* for the guidebook, and most players deliberately offload it so they can
*keep dying*. (He returns in *Chu-Teng* as the Star Palace's cook.)

### What spaces do narratively
Every space is body-as-psyche: the Tree of Life is accessed through a king's
*mouth* (via sneeze); the Helix Palace sits *in the palm of a king's hand*;
the computer that registers life is *inside a fire tower*; the Palace of
Dreaming is entered *through a waterfall*. Rooms in the Helix Palace are
"alive as well" — the Room of Appetite force-feeds you gourmet food until you
explode. Space never decorates; it *argues*.

## 6. Comedy

Sato's humor is **cosmic deadpan** — the joke is never signaled; the universe
states it flatly and moves on:

- **Pang-xie**, a crab who severs his own legs because they're his favorite
  dish; they grow back. Drunk, "he'll feed you all, one after another, with
  his private cuisine."
- **Retsu the ant**: a three-act farce — born with the noble duty "help the
  troubled," gets distracted by his own thoughts, is thrown at a king's face,
  dies, and thereby *succeeds*.
- **Jin**: a whole playable life that collapses into slag during the loading
  screen of birth. The joke is on the concept of "chosen one" quests.
- **Sha hands the instruments to King-gyou**, who promised a gem — and
  King-gyou just takes them and gives nothing. Desire as theft, without
  commentary.
- **Moku-gyou believes the birdlike creature living on his head is the only
  thing saving him from being devoured by ants** — and the ants are real.
- **Zen's gag**: click the face frozen in the ice wall and it explodes. He
  stays exploded. The wiki: "this does nothing practical."
- **The bronze creature that runs across the screen saying "Fun!"** —
  unexplained, filed under "it's in the guidebook."
- **A three-minded creature appears, has an existential crisis, and dies
  within moments of meeting you.**
- **Tou's inferiority complex** about his aerodynamic hole-in-the-body,
  documented in the official encyclopedia like a stat block.

**How the absurdity serves the spiritual themes:** the comedy IS the
philosophy. Every joke is a koan about non-attachment: Jin's greed is
structurally unsound; Retsu's enlightenment comes through failure; Zen's duty
is fulfilled by dying at it; the Room of Immortality is the only true Game
Over. The game laughs *with* the doctrine of impermanence — clinging (to
items, to lives, to logic) is the punchline, and letting go is the win. The
Obscuritory: "This isn't randomness for its own sake. This is a Zen parable
about balance in life, finding your purpose, and discovering yourself."

obscuritory.com · myabandonware.com

## Bible Integration Points

- **Forehead-portal canon → the orifice/head-world geography.** Tong-Nou
  proves the device works as a sustained setting: a head the cast physically
  *climbs into*, each orifice a doorway, each interior space arguing a thesis.
  Steal the specifics: the forehead/ear entry isn't symbolic decoration — on
  Tong-Nou, clicking the head itself triggers bodily reactions (drills,
  eyeball-vomit, screaming-eyeball cheeks). Mars's head should *react* to
  visitors the way a living island does. (Informs: Bible A forehead-portal
  canon; environment design.)
- **Four-land taxonomy → elemental identity per reality.** Eastern Mind's
  deepest structural trick isn't four lands — it's that every land rhymes on
  three axes: **element, music, and soul-force** (Life=Wood, Time=Fire,
  Desire=Metal, Dreaming=Water). God Molecule's Life/Time/Desire/Dreaming +
  Central Mountain should each carry a locked elemental signature + musical
  palette, not just a theme. Sato's per-land scoring is the production model
  for Mars's inner worlds. (Informs: infinite-world expansion; visual/music
  bible.)
- **Death-as-transition (not reset).** The mechanics to steal: (1)
  progression and inventory *survive* death; (2) death is a *menu*
  (eyes/nose/mouth selection — Kevin could "re-roll" which reality Mars
  enters); (3) lives complete *by* dying (Retsu, Zen, Gyou); (4) the only true
  failure is *refusing* to die (the immortality slideshow). An episode where
  Mars or Kevin "dies" plays as a transition beat with a new-perspective
  payoff; the unforgivable sin in Mars's mind is clinging/stasis. (Informs:
  Bible B law 9; transition mechanics.)
- **Nine-lives season arc → the torn-king structure.** The ending's
  masterstroke: **Tou-gyou was torn into eight pieces scattered across the
  world = the eight reincarnation lives**, and the soul-fragments (magatamas)
  were stolen by the four land-kings for their own use. A season arc writes
  itself: nine missions, each mission *is* a piece of a shattered central
  figure, each land's ruler exploiting the fragments. The "twist" — the
  devouring island actually purifies souls — is the season-finale reversal
  template. (Informs: season-arc construction.)
- **Trickster hub = Kevin's territory.** Mai-chiu and the Phantom Marketplace
  are the blueprint: a lone merchant, barter-only, published-but-absurd
  exchange rates, a shop that appears/vanishes with his moods, trades that
  function as *alternate solutions* (the amulet-for-guidebook swap = trading
  safety for knowledge). Kevin's trickster marketplace runs on the same rules:
  **no money accepted, every trade has a catch, the merchant is the only
  permanent resident.** (Informs: Kevin's role; recurring location design.)
- **"Born with the words of duty" → character-title device.** Every creature
  ships with its thesis printed on it (Retsu: "help the troubled"; Zen:
  "notify all change"). Give each world-visitor or soul-fragment a one-line
  *duty* — it instantly characterizes and instantly generates plot. (Informs:
  character-writing standard.)
- **The Illustrated Book → the show's in-world field guide.** A dry,
  faux-encyclopedic book with Likes/Dislikes that is simultaneously the hint
  system, the comedy engine, and the lore bible. A "Marshead Field Guide"
  interstitial segment carries the same triple duty. (Informs: interstitial
  format; series mythology.)
- **Music pipeline (game ⇄ album).** Sato's *Transmigration* album and the game
  fed each other; the US release bundled album tracks. The God Molecule
  soundtrack should be produced as a standalone album *during* production,
  with tracks debuting in-episode and the album as a revenue artifact.
  (Informs: music production; monetization-adjacent.)
- **Non-tutorial teaching.** Eastern Mind never explains itself; it teaches
  through the Mirror's riddles, NPC quizzes, and death. The show's equivalent:
  Mars and Kevin never explain a world's rules — the world *demonstrates*
  them, usually by punishing the wrong assumption. No lore-dump dialogue; the
  absurdity is the exposition. (Informs: Bible B law 13; writing standard.)
- **Multi-medium authorship model.** Sato (concept/art/music) + Nishikawa
  (dream-text writing) is the split to copy: a visual auteur and a
  *dream-journal* writer. "Dream-journal writer" is now a defined role —
  someone who writes the impossible as mundane. (Informs: production staffing
  model.)

### Open questions
1. No genuine Sato interview exists (HG101 states this explicitly) — his
   method comes from his 1993 book via HG101's quote and the Vice line.
2. The transmigration picker: compu-lsd documents 2 eyes × 2 noses × 2 mouths
   = 8 creatures; Wikipedia says three of each — the 2×2×2 math matches the
   creature count, so 2×2×2 is reported.
3. The 49-day timer is narrative framing only — no mechanical countdown is
   documented in any walkthrough.
